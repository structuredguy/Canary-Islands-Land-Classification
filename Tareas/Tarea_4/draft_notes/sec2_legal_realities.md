# 2. Canary Islands Urban Law & Scale Realities

---

<a id="sub2_1"></a>
## 2.1 Legal Outlets for Trapped Floor Area (`TAU`, Subsoil & Attics)

When a 2-floor height limit strands `11,400 m2c` (`S1`) or `33,000 m2c` (`S2`) (see [§1.2 Scenario Deficits](sec1_statutory_model.md#sub1_2)), not every subterranean or roof volume resolves the legal deficit:

1. **`TAU` (*Transferencia de Aprovechamiento Urbanístico*):** The primary statutory outlet — building what physically fits in 2 stories on site and transferring remaining weighted development rights to a designated higher-height receiving sector under the *Plan General*.
2. **Strictly Non-Computable Mandatory Garages (*Sótanos*):** If municipal ordinances exclude mandatory subterranean parking (`>= 1` stall/dwelling, Ley 4/2017 Art. 138) from `E_teórica`, **any** developer (even with `0 m2c` spare buildability) can excavate them. Therefore, non-computable garages improve **commercial marketability** (keeping 100% of both above-ground floors as living space) rather than absorbing trapped `m2c`.
3. **When Subsoil & Roofs DO Absorb Spare `E_teórica`:**
   - **Above-mandatory basements / *semisótanos*:** Under strict "mandatory-only" *ordenanzas*, a 2nd garage bay, cellar (*bodega*), or walk-out *semisótano* on sloped terrain computes toward `E_teórica`.
   - ***Bajo cubierta* (pitched roof lofts):** Keeping roof pitch ($\sim 30^\circ\text{–}35^\circ$) within statutory cornice height avoids triggering a prohibited 3rd facade floor, while interior zones with clear headroom above $\sim 1.50\text{–}1.80\text{ m}$ compute against `E_teórica` — absorbing the `S1` (`0.47`-floor) overhang cleanly where pitched roofs are permitted.

## 2.2 Municipal Lucrative Cession (`10%–15%` PMS)

Under **Ley 4/2017 Art. 44.c** (`10%` statutory cession; within the **`5%–15%`** range permitted by **RDL 7/2015 Art. 18.1.b**):

- **What Is Ceded:** **Serviced net residential plots free of urbanization charges** (*parcelas urbanizadas libres de cargas de urbanización*) transferred to the *Patrimonio Municipal de Suelo* (PMS) — **not** finished buildings.
- **Why Physical Sector Geometry Stays Unchanged:** Municipal plots still sit inside total $S_{\text{neta}}$, so sector-wide $S_{\text{cesiones}}$ and $N_{\text{plantas}}$ in Chapter 1 do not shift (unlike `Tarea_3`, which specifically counted promoter-only plots).
- **Financial Asymmetry:** The promoter receives **`90%` of revenue-bearing floor area** (`0.90 * E_teórica`) while bearing **`100%` of sector urbanization costs** ($S_{\text{bruta}}$).

> **Side Jump &rarr; Folding PMS into Social Housing:** In mixed sectors, the `10%` public PMS cession satisfies part of the total `40%` VPO buildability budget. [**Jump to §4.1 Decoupled VPO Frontier &rarr;**](sec4_mixed_vpo_synthesis.md#sub4_1)

## 2.3 Intensive Ratios vs. Extensive Sector Scale

Because `BCR` and `Gross FAR` are **intensive ratios**, the core mass-balance equations yield identical curves whether a site is `0.5 ha` or `500 ha`. Real-world development breaks scale invariance through four extensive mechanisms:

1. **Small-Site Plot Quantization:** On compact infill parcels (`0.5–1.5 ha`), losing a fraction just under `700 m2s` causes sharp percentage jumps in plot yield compared with a `6 ha` sector.
2. **Market Absorption & Carry Costs:** Flooding local demand with hundreds of simultaneous units depresses clearing prices and compounds financing carry over multi-year buildouts.
3. **Fixed Infrastructure vs. Threshold Mandates:** Small tracts suffer high fixed utility tie-in costs per unit, whereas mega-developments cross statutory thresholds triggering *Sistemas Generales* and mandatory VPO reserves.
4. **Unit Footprint Geometry:** `700 m2s` is strictly a **lower statutory bound**, allowing heterogeneous parcel sizes (compact attached lots alongside larger villa lots) within the same *Proyecto de Reparcelación*.
